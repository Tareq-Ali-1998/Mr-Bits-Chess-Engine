♟️ Threefold Repetition

A clear guide to repeated positions in chess
🟦 Recognize it · 🟨 Claim it · 🟩 Understand the exceptions

🟦 What counts?

Under FIDE rules, three occurrences of the same position allow the player to move to claim a draw. They need not be consecutive, and the moves leading to them may differ.

Positions match only when all four conditions match:

Check Required match
♟️ Pieces Same piece types and colors on the same squares
🔄 Turn Same player to move
🏰 Castling Same castling rights, even if castling is currently blocked
⚡ En passant Same legally available en passant captures

🟨 How to claim

If the position has just appeared for the third time: claim with the arbiter before moving.

If your intended move would create it: record that move on your scoresheet, then declare your intention and claim with the arbiter before playing it. The recorded move cannot be changed.

Pause the clock when claiming. Claim before touching a piece to move or capture.

🟩 Threefold vs. fivefold

Occurrences Result
3 Draw upon a correct claim; opponent’s agreement is unnecessary
5 Automatic draw; no claim required

💡 Example: returning home

From the standard starting position:

1. Nf3 Nf6
2. Ng1 Ng8    ← Starting position: occurrence 2
3. Nf3 Nf6
4. Ng1 Ng8    ← Starting position: occurrence 3

The starting position itself was occurrence 1. After 4...Ng8, White can claim. Alternatively, Black can record 4...Ng8 and claim before playing it.

🟥 Common misconceptions

  • Count positions, not repeated moves.
  • Returning a king or rook does not restore lost castling rights.
  • Repetition does not prove that progress or a win is impossible; it provides a way to end recurring play.
  • Announcing a claim only to the opponent is not the formal FIDE procedure.

📚 Official source: FIDE Laws of Chess, Articles 9.2, 9.4–9.6.
